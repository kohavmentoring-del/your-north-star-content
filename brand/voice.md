# Your North Star — Script Brief (Kohav's brand)

## Read this first
- One story per script. Never combine stories (birth, trampoline, dad, NYC, Miriam) into one video. Each story is its own reel.
- Use her words. "Her stories" below is Kohav speaking, lightly cleaned from voice transcripts. Keep her phrasing, her rhythm and her small imperfections. Cut and reorder only. Don't polish her into "content voice".
- No AI sound. Avoid neat parallel lines, "not X, it's Y" reveals, one-word dramatic sentences stacked up, and generic motivational closers. If a line could appear on anyone's account, cut it.
- Hooks: short, no dashes, high emotional punch, and they sound like something she'd actually say to a friend.
- Don't preach. She tells the story and the feeling. The lesson comes from her life, said once, in her words.
- English is her second language, and that's fine. Natural non-native phrasing is part of her voice. Only fix grammar that would confuse a viewer.

## The brand in short
- Who: Kohav, 26, a first-year university student who moved to New York alone and is starting over.
- Message (her words): "If you truly believe in something, it will happen." "Once you have the right mindset, miracles can happen." "Positivity and hope and belief can truly change the trajectory of life."
- Also core: kindness. Caring about other people, especially lonely people, not only about ourselves and our phones.
- Audience: young people (late teens to mid 20s, mostly women) who feel stuck, don't believe in themselves, and know they could do more. Plus New Yorkers looking for community. She speaks to everyone and never says "ladies".
- Pillars: (1) her story, against all odds; (2) mindset and brain tools, always opened with a moment from her life; (3) documenting her journey in NYC and trying things badly ("attempting to suck"); (4) kindness and connection, including Miriam and conversations with strangers.
- Formats: storytelling reels, talking head, voiceover with B-roll. Edited with a warm cinematic look, captions and animations.
- Not allowed: selling or paid promotion. Until May 2027 this account only builds a community.

## Her stories, in her own words

1. **Born at one kilogram** — "I was born three months earlier before I should have been born. And I always felt like the story of my birth is a story of survival and resilience. I was born one kilogram, and then I had a really hard virus that killed all of the preemies at my hospital. I got the virus and I went down to 700 grams, and I had a life-saving operation in my stomach and I was saved. So it was like a miracle."

2. **The trampoline** — "I was not invited to a birthday party when I was 10 years old, and I decided to come anyway. And when I arrived, the mom opened the door and she's like, I don't think my daughter invited you. And I said, that's okay. I think I can come anyway. They didn't invite me because I wasn't cool enough, because I was bullied. And when I got there, this girl said, we don't have any space for you, but if you want, you can sleep outside in the cold on the trampoline. And she expected me to go home crying. And what I did, I said, you know what? I'm going to do it. So only to prove her wrong, I slept on the trampoline the entire night. As a 10 years old, think of the amount of inner strength this kid has. It's crazy. And I didn't tell my mom that for two weeks. And when I did, she was so mad at that woman and at her daughter. But I was so proud of myself."

3. **Jealous of my younger self** — "I grew up as a weird kid. I was a Lady Gaga fan. I battled with bullying, kids that did not like me and kids that laughed at me because I was so different. And what is so interesting is that I was so brave. I was so devoted to who I truly was. I did not care. And I truly think this quality made me who I am today. But sometimes, if we're honest, I'm jealous of her. I want to be more like my childhood me, that was so blind to everyone's opinion. I just did what the heck I wanted."

4. **My dad's coma** — "My parents had a hit and run on one of the first times I ever slept over at a friend's house. And that completely changed my life. My dad was almost three months in a coma. My mom broke every bone. They were a year and a half in hospitals. And I had to be removed from my home and go live at my uncle with my dog. And then I had to go live at my grandma's with my dog. I was always with a bag, always feeling like a gypsy, going from place to place, having no parents for such a long time. The doctors told my mom he's never going to wake up. My mom believed in him. She prayed. She said God has a bigger purpose for him. She was so positive the whole time he was in a coma, she did not let any other person's belief change her mind. And she was right. He woke up. Then the doctor said, okay, he woke up, he won't be able to move. And then he moved. Okay, he won't be able to talk. He spoke. He won't be able to walk. He started walking. So it truly taught me how much our brain and our mind is so much more capable than any limitation we can put on it."

5. **Starting over in New York at 26** — "I'm in New York City at 26, starting over. I broke up with my boyfriend of five years, who I thought I was gonna marry. And then I started from scratch. Without a relationship, without friends, without anyone I know in the city besides one person. And I took a bet on myself. Moving to New York was my biggest dream since I was 10."

6. **Miriam** — "Miriam is an elderly woman I met in Toronto while doing trick-or-treat. And I learned that she's alone, and since then I kind of adopted her, and she became very, very close to me. And now she was sick and I flew to Toronto to see her. And it's not about people saying, oh, she's so amazing. I want to show how much caring about other people is so important. Not only caring about ourselves and our phone. There are so many elderly people, so many lonely people, and I want to help make that smaller."

7. **Mentoring** — "I did a gap year in Toronto, then I started mentoring in that gap year program. I mentored more than 80 people in four years. I helped them navigate their feelings, their dreams, their goals. That's when I became really invested in how to change people's perspective and mindset."

## How she talks (use this, don't invent a voice)
- Long, flowing sentences that build. She doesn't talk in clipped fragments.
- Words she uses: "truly", "so", "crazy", "honestly", "if we're honest", "think of".
- She talks straight to the viewer ("think of...", "you need to learn...").
- She's openly emotional and proud ("I was so proud of myself") without being polished.
- Her faith shows up naturally when it's part of the story (her mom praying).
